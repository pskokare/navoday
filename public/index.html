About Doctor

Meet Dr. Pradipkumar Tupere

Proctologist and General Surgeon. MBBS, MS (General Surgery).

Dr. Pradipkumar Tupere is a proctologist and general surgeon who treats piles (Haemorrhoids), anal fissure, anal fistula, pilonidal sinus and other anorectal conditions. He completed his MBBS at Shri Bhausaheb Hire Government Medical College, Dhule, and his MS in General Surgery at B.J. Medical College, Pune.

His training includes stapler surgery for haemorrhoids in Italy, laser surgery for haemorrhoids and fistula in Austria, and VAAFT, injection sclerotherapy and CLIS techniques in Mumbai. He gave an international paper presentation on treatment modalities for fistula-in-ano in 2014.

His experience includes Jehangir Hospital (Pune), YCM Hospital (Pimpri), Lilavati Hospital and L.H. Hiranandani Hospital (Mumbai), and CRHP, Jamkhed.

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers to what patients ask before a first visit.

See a doctor if you notice bleeding while passing stool, a lump near the anus, pain or itching that does not settle, or symptoms that keep coming back. Bleeding should always be examined, because other conditions can look similar.

No. Early-stage piles (Haemorrhoids) are often managed with diet, lifestyle changes and medicines. A procedure is considered when symptoms continue or the piles (Haemorrhoids) are more advanced, and Dr. Tupere advises the right option after examining you.

Piles are swollen blood vessels in the anal canal. A fissure is a small tear in the lining of the anus. A fistula is an abnormal channel between the anal canal and the skin nearby. They can cause similar symptoms, such as pain and bleeding, so an examination is needed to tell them apart.

Both cause pain and bleeding near the anus, but they are different problems. A fissure is a small tear that usually hurts sharply during and after a motion. Piles are swollen veins and often bleed with little pain. Only an examination can confirm which one you have.

Most anal fistulas do not heal with medicines or creams alone, because the tunnel needs a procedure to close it. The right method depends on where the fistula runs, and Dr. Tupere decides this after an examination.
